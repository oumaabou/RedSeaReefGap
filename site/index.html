RedSeaReefGap

Where does the public biodiversity record for Red Sea reef fish actually have data, and where is it silent? A GBIF sampling gap analysis, built in R.

GitHub repository · Full methodology and limitations

The question

This project looks at six common Red Sea reef fish families (surgeonfishes, tunas and mackerels, parrotfishes, jacks, groupers, and snappers) through the lens of the Global Biodiversity Information Facility (GBIF). Rather than mapping where these fish live, it maps where they have been recorded, which for data this heavy on citizen science turns out to be a very different, and much more revealing, question.

Headline numbers

70 to 85% of every family's GBIF records come from a single country, Egypt, concentrated on two Sinai dive sites
70 to 82% of dated records are from 2015 onward, tracking iNaturalist's growth rather than fish abundance
Under 5% typical combined share of records from Sudan, Eritrea, Yemen and Djibouti per family

In practice, a trend read directly off raw GBIF record counts for these families is, to a large extent, a trend in dive tourism and app usage, not necessarily a trend in the fish themselves.

Method, in brief

All figures come from live queries against the GBIF Occurrence API, restricted to a Red Sea bounding box (12 to 30°N, 32 to 44°E). Country and year breakdowns use GBIF's own facet counts, which cover every matching record, not a sample. The full R pipeline (clean, spatial gap, temporal gap, map) runs automatically on GitHub Actions on every push, so these charts are regenerated from a real, reproducible run rather than asserted. See the Scope and limitations section of the README for the caveats (GBIF only, bounding box edge cases, citizen science bias).
